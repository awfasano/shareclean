What is EXIF data? Photo metadata explained

Every photo you take can carry a hidden layer of information called metadata. EXIF is the best-known kind. Here is what it contains, why it matters, and how to remove it.

What EXIF data is

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is information your camera writes into a photo file alongside the picture. You do not see it when you look at the photo, but apps and tools can read it. Related formats such as IPTC and manufacturer-specific metadata can be stored in the same file.

What it can include

  • GPS coordinates and altitude, if location was on for the camera
  • Camera make, model and serial details
  • Lens and shooting settings such as aperture, shutter speed and ISO
  • The date and time the photo was taken
  • Software version and Apple-specific metadata

Why it matters

If a photo with GPS coordinates reaches someone, they may be able to see where it was taken, such as your home or your child's school. Timestamps can reveal your routine. Some apps and sites remove metadata when you upload, and others may not, so it is safer not to rely on it.

Questions

Can I see the EXIF data of my photo on iPhone?

Yes. In the Photos app, swipe up on a photo or tap the info button to see some details such as date, camera and location on the map. Not every metadata field is shown there.

Does removing EXIF data change how the photo looks?

No. Stripping metadata does not affect image quality. If you also choose to resize or compress in ShareClean, quality depends on the settings you choose, and you can preview the result.

Does ShareClean upload my photos?

No. All processing happens on your device. There are no accounts, servers, advertising SDKs or analytics trackers.
